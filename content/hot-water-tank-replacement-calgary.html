Short answer: Calgary companies publish installed prices for a standard gas tank replacement in the range of roughly $1,800–$3,500, with electric at the lower end and power-vent gas at the upper end. Treat that as a planning range and nothing more — every published figure in this city is a company quoting itself, there is no independent source, and the thing that actually decides your number is the venting, not the tank.

You've either got water on the floor or a tank with a date sticker you've finally gone and read, and you want a number before you start phoning people. The frustrating part is that the prices online are all published by the companies selling the work, and they vary by more than a thousand dollars for what sounds like the same job. Here's where those numbers come from, what genuinely moves them on a Calgary house, and what to ask so three quotes become comparable instead of confusing.

Where the published numbers come from

Start with the honest disclosure, because it changes how you read everything else. There is no authoritative public source for what a hot water tank costs to replace in Calgary. No City department publishes it, no trade association publishes it, no consumer body audits it. Every dollar figure you will find, including the range at the top of this page, traces back to a plumbing or HVAC company's own website. That doesn't make them useless — where independent companies land in the same territory, the territory is probably about right. It does mean you should know whose numbers you're looking at.

The venting decides your price, not the tank

Two identical 50-gallon tanks can be a $2,400 job and a $3,600 job on the same street. The difference is almost never the box. It's how the exhaust leaves the building, which means you can work out roughly which conversation you're in before anyone comes over.

Atmospheric — the cheap case

A plain metal flue pipe rising off the tank into a chimney, no fan. If yours is atmospheric and the flue is still sound and correctly sized, a like-for-like swap is the simplest version of this job — so expect a quote near the bottom of the range, and push back on one near the top.

Power vent — the expensive case

A blower on top of the tank pushing exhaust out through a plastic pipe in a side wall. The unit itself costs more and it needs an electrical outlet beside it, so it lands higher — but it frees you from the chimney entirely, which is what makes it the answer when the chimney is the problem.

Switching between them — the surprise

Going from atmospheric to power vent adds a wall penetration, new venting and power. That's the quote that comes back a thousand dollars over what your neighbour paid, and it isn't padding — it's a different job, so ask for it to be shown as its own line rather than folded into the tank price.

The switch happens more here than most people expect, and usually for one reason: the furnace went first. Plenty of Calgary homes built before the 1990s share a masonry chimney between the furnace and the water heater. When that furnace is replaced with a modern high-efficiency unit venting out a side wall, the tank is left alone on a flue sized for two. Somebody eventually has to deal with that, and it is frequently the person replacing the water heater — often without being warned it was coming.

If your furnace was replaced in the last few years and your tank still vents into the chimney, ask specifically about the flue when you get quotes. It's the most common way a Calgary tank replacement turns out bigger than budgeted, and far better found at the quote stage than on installation day.

What else moves the number

  • Where the tank actually sits. Beside the stairs on bare concrete is a straightforward carry. Wedged behind a finished wall, or down a tight turn, means more time and sometimes drywall — so answer the access question honestly on the phone rather than letting it surface on the invoice.
  • Whether the gas line is right for the new unit. A larger burner draws more gas, and the line has to be assessed against everything else on the meter — furnace, range, fireplace, garage heater — not just the tank alone. If it needs resizing, that's separate work by a licensed gasfitter. How gas permits work in Calgary →
  • The condition of what it connects to. Seized shut-off valves, corroded unions and old flexible connectors get replaced while the system is drained, because doing it later means draining it again. Small money at the time, annoying money afterwards.
  • When you're calling. Demand isn't flat. A tank that fails during a cold snap competes with every other emergency in the city that week, and you're choosing from whoever can come rather than whoever quoted best. How after-hours plumbing pricing works →
  • Removal and disposal of the old tank. Usually a small line, occasionally omitted from a quote entirely. Check that it's in there, because a drained tank left in your utility room is still your problem.

We can't point you to a public benchmark for how long the job should take — nobody publishes one for Calgary, and any confident number you read online is somebody's marketing. What you can do is ask each shop what they've scheduled for it. Three answers of "most of a morning" and one of "an hour" tells you something useful.

Does it need a permit?

Often, but not for the reason most pages give. The City of Calgary states that a plumbing permit is not required to replace existing fixtures, and on the water side a tank going back into the same place on the same connections is a fixture replacement. What puts a permit in play is usually the gas: the connection, the venting, or a change in the type of heater.

Source: The City of Calgary — Homeowner electrical and plumbing permits (current as of August 2026)

And the gas is the part you can't do yourself regardless of how handy you are. A Calgary homeowner cannot pull a gas permit unless they personally hold Alberta first- or second-class gasfitter certification, live at the address, and apply in person with proof. That's why the DIY route collapses on a gas tank even though Alberta's owner exemption lets you do your own plumbing — gasfitting is a separate certification from plumbing, and one doesn't cover the other. Why Calgary tanks come out earlier than the box promised

This is measurable rather than sales talk. The City publishes hardness figures for both treatment plants: Bearspaw runs roughly 141–200 mg/L of calcium carbonate through the year and Glenmore runs roughly 181–274 mg/L. The City doesn't publish grains per gallon, so we converted it — divide by 17.1 and that's about 8–12 gpg on the Bearspaw side and 10.6–16.0 on the Glenmore side, which is "hard" to "very hard."

That mineral settles out in the bottom of the tank, where it insulates the burner from the water it's trying to heat and makes the whole thing work harder for the same shower. It's the main reason Calgary tanks tend to underperform the lifespan printed on the label, and it's worth knowing before you buy: the premium tank isn't buying you the warranty years unless the water it holds is being managed. Before you replace, make sure you need to

Replacement gets recommended faster than it needs to be, and a tank is an expensive thing to buy for a small fault. No hot water at all is very often a pilot, thermocouple or gas valve — or on an electric tank an element or thermostat — all repairs. Water near the base can be condensation, a discharge from the temperature and pressure valve, or a fitting rather than a rusted-through shell. The signs that genuinely mean replacement are rusty hot water at the taps, heavy rumbling from sediment, and moisture coming from the tank body itself. If you're not sure which you've got, work through the no-hot-water checks first — five minutes that occasionally saves a couple of thousand dollars.

How to make three quotes comparable

  1. Photograph your setup before you call. The top of the tank, the vent pipe, the gas line and the rating plate. Sending the same four photos to everyone means you're getting quotes on the same job instead of three guesses.
  2. Ask each of them what vent type they're quoting. If one has quoted a power vent and the others haven't, you've found your price gap immediately — and you can then ask the cheaper ones what they're planning to do about the chimney.
  3. Get the tank, labour, venting, disposal and permit as separate lines. A single number is fine to sign but not to compare. Separate lines show you which shop is expensive on labour and which is expensive on the unit.
  4. Ask who warranties what, and for how long. The tank warranty comes from the manufacturer. The labour warranty comes from the shop, and it's the one that varies. Get both in writing.
  5. Ask what happens if they find something once they start. Corroded valves, a failed flue, an undersized line. You want to know whether that's a phone call to you or a line added to the invoice — before they start.
