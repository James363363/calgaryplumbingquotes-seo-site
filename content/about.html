What "licensed" actually means here

Almost every plumbing company in this city puts "licensed and insured" on its homepage, and almost none of them explain what that means. It's worth knowing, because it changes what questions you should ask.

Plumber is a compulsory certification trade in Alberta. That isn't a marketing distinction — it means the person doing the work legally must hold a journeyperson certificate, a recognized trade certificate, or be a registered apprentice working under one. Getting there takes four apprenticeship periods, each requiring 1,560 hours of on-the-job experience plus eight weeks of classroom time. A Red Seal endorsement requires passing both the Plumber and the Gasfitter Class B interprovincial exams.

Source: Alberta Tradesecrets — Plumber trade profile

The part that catches people out: gasfitting is a separate ticket from plumbing. A Red Seal plumber is not automatically a gasfitter. So if your job involves a gas water heater, a range line, a BBQ line or a garage heater, "we're licensed plumbers" is not the answer to the question you're actually asking. Ask who is coming and what they hold. We've written the full explainer on Alberta's licensing rules here, including what you're legally allowed to do yourself in your own home.

How we price work — and why we publish it

There is no regulator, association or public body that publishes what plumbers charge in Calgary. Every number you'll find online, including on competitors' sites and including any range we quote, is a company's own self-report. We think you should know that before you compare quotes.

  • Scoped replacement work is quoted free. A hot water tank, a backwater valve, a basement rough-in — we come out, look at what's actually there, and give you a written price. You can take that number to someone else and we won't be offended.
  • Diagnosis is billed, and we tell you the number before we drive over. If nobody knows why your drain keeps backing up, someone has to spend an hour with a camera finding out. That's real work. What we won't do is call it a "free estimate" and then produce a service call charge once the van is in your driveway.
  • If you don't proceed, you'll already know what you owe. Ask us on the phone — ask any plumber on the phone — "what do I owe if you come out and I decide not to go ahead?" A company that won't answer that plainly is telling you something.
  • We pull the permit when the job needs one. That's a real line item and we'd rather show it than bury it. It also means your paperwork is clean if the work ever surfaces during a home sale or an insurance claim, which is precisely when unpermitted work becomes expensive.

How the guides on this site are written

Most plumbing "resources" pages are keyword filler with a phone number attached. We've tried to do something different, and it's fair for you to know exactly how, because that's the only way you can judge whether to trust any of it.

  1. Primary sources first, linked inline. Anything we state about City of Calgary rules, permit fees, water hardness, utility rates or Alberta trade licensing comes from The City of Calgary, Alberta Tradesecrets, or the relevant utility — and we link it directly under the claim so you can click through and verify. If a competitor states a fact with no source, that's your cue to be sceptical of them too.
  2. We say when we don't know. There are things we could not verify: whether The City charges to thaw a frozen service line, whether discharging a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is permitted here, which treatment plant serves which neighbourhood, whether there's a compensation process for City-caused backups. Rather than filling those gaps with confident-sounding guesses, the pages say we couldn't confirm it and point you at 311. That's less satisfying to read and much more useful.
  3. Prices are ranges, and we say where they came from. No fabricated price lists. Where a figure originates from another company's published page, we say so and link it. Where it's our own arithmetic on City-published data, we show the working.
  4. Content production is AI-assisted, and we're saying so on purpose. Research, drafting and structuring on this site are AI-assisted, with the facts checked against primary sources and the trade judgement coming from the business. Google's own guidance asks publishers to be transparent about how content is produced, and we'd rather volunteer it than have you find out.
  5. Pages get dated. Every page carries a last-reviewed date. Permit fees change every January 1. Utility rates change. Programs come and go. A plumbing page with no date on it is asking you to trust that nothing has changed since it was written, which is not a reasonable thing to ask.

What we don't do

Knowing where someone stops is more useful than a list of everything they claim to handle.

  • Furnaces, air conditioning and ductwork. Different trade, different ticket. We'll tell you who to call.
  • Foundation waterproofing and exterior weeping tile replacement. That's excavation and waterproofing work, not plumbing. If your problem is water coming through the wall rather than out of a pipe, we'll say so instead of selling you a sump pump.
  • Commercial and new-build construction. We do residential service and renovation work.
  • Restoration and drying after a flood. We stop the water. Restoration companies dry the house. They're separate jobs and separate insurance conversations.
