Short answer: converting a Calgary house from a storage tank to tankless is not an appliance swap. It is a gas job, a venting job, an electrical job and a drainage job that happen to end with a water heater on the wall. Your gas line, your chimney and your floor drain decide the price — not the brand on the box. Calgary companies publish installed figures around $3,500 to $7,000, self-reported and unaudited, and that gap is almost entirely the four things below.

Still deciding whether to do it at all? We published the annual energy arithmetic on tank versus tankless running costs, working shown.

Four things change, and each one has a price attached

1. The gas supply, which is the big one

A storage tank draws roughly 40,000 BTU an hour, slowly, reheating a reservoir. A tankless unit has no reservoir, so it makes the heat the moment you open the tap. The condensing units sold here are rated up to 199,900 BTU/h — about five times the demand, all at once, through whatever pipe is already there.

And it only works inside a narrow pressure band: 3.5 to 10.5 inches of water column at the inlet for natural gas. Not at 2 a.m. with nothing else running — at dinner time, furnace firing, range on. An undersized line does not stop the unit starting; it makes it misbehave on exactly the coldest mornings you bought it for.

Source: Navien — NPE Series installation manual and Rinnai — N Series residential condensing installation and operation manual.

For many Calgary homes that means a larger-diameter run from the meter, sometimes a question about the meter itself. 2. The venting, which cannot reuse your chimney

This is the surprise, because the old flue is sitting right there looking serviceable. A condensing unit pulls so much heat out of the exhaust that what leaves is cool and wet rather than hot and buoyant — it will not rise up a masonry or B-vent chimney, and it will corrode one. The manufacturers list Schedule 40 PVC, CPVC or approved polypropylene, out through an exterior wall, with maximum lengths that shorten with every elbow.

Source: Navien — NPE Series installation manual (approved vent materials and maximum vent lengths).

So somebody is cutting a hole in your wall, and where matters: a sidewall termination here needs clearance from windows, walkways and anything that minds being rained on by warm vapour at −25 °C. Pick the wall with the installer standing beside you.

3. The condensate, which has to go somewhere legal

That same efficiency produces liquid water, continuously, and it is not neutral. Navien puts the condensate's pH at 3 to 5 — acidic enough that the manual sells a neutralizer kit and says to replace the media once pH drops below 6.0. So the unit needs a correctly sloped drain within reach, which usually means it wants to live near the floor drain or laundry standpipe, not in the corner where the tank was. If the drain is across the room, that line is real work and belongs on the quote.

Source: Navien — NPE Series installation manual (condensate pH and neutralizer media replacement).

4. The electrical, which the old tank never needed

A gas tank with a standing pilot runs on nothing. A tankless unit needs 120 volts for controls, fan and ignition, so if there is no outlet where it is going, you need one — and your endless hot water is now tied to the grid. In a Calgary outage you have neither hot water nor a tank full of it left over.

Source: Rinnai — N Series residential condensing installation and operation manual (120 V AC electrical supply).

The chimney question to ask out loud. If your tank shared a flue with an older mid-efficiency furnace, pulling the tank out leaves that furnace venting alone up a chimney sized for two appliances. We could not find a plain-language City or Alberta publication settling what has to happen next, so we will not state a rule we cannot cite — but it is a known scenario, it is not free, and it is the classic mid-job surprise. Get it addressed in writing on the quote; call 311 for the City's own read.

The hard-water problem no Calgary tankless page prints a number for

Every tankless page in this city mentions hard water. None we read put the manufacturer's limit beside the City's measurements — odd, because both are published and they do not agree.

Navien and Rinnai both specify a maximum total hardness of 200 mg/L, about 12 grains per gallon. Rinnai is blunt about the consequence: in an area known for hard water or scale build-up, the water must be treated. Navien ties water outside its stated quality range to warranty exposure.

Sources: Navien — NPE Series installation manual; Rinnai — N Series residential condensing installation and operation manual.

Read the right-hand column again. Glenmore water sat above the manufacturers' ceiling in all four quarters of 2025, and Bearspaw reached it twice. That does not mean tankless units fail here — plenty run for years. It means that across a large part of this city, installing one with nothing in front of it puts the appliance outside its maker's published water spec on day one. That is a warranty conversation to have before you sign, not after a heat exchanger fails.

Which plant serves your street decides where you sit, and the City publishes the hardness data but not a neighbourhood-by-plant map — so ask 311 rather than letting anyone guess. On the hard side the options are treating the incoming water or flushing far more often than the brochure implies: water softener installation covers the first, the Calgary hard water page covers why this kills equipment here. Either way, fit isolation valves at install time — cheap in the original job, irritating to retrofit, and without them every flush is a plumbing call.

Permits: this job triggers them, and you can't pull your own

The City names three conditions requiring a permit for water heater work: changes to the gas lines upstream of the water heater shut-off valve, changes to the venting system's size or design, and a change to the type of water heater. A tank-to-tankless conversion does all three at once — one of the few water heater jobs with nothing to argue about.

Source: The City of Calgary — Homeowner electrical and plumbing permits.

The part that surprises DIY-minded owners: this is gas work, and homeowners cannot take out a gas permit on their own house unless they are a first- or second-class gasfitter. Plumbing and gasfitting are separate tickets in Alberta — what you can legally do yourself is here, and the full permit triggers are here.

Source: The City of Calgary — Plumbing and gas permits: "If you are a homeowner, you may apply for all permit types except for a gas permit."

Ranking Calgary pages describe the permit for this job as "$100 to $150." That is the homeowner plumbing permit — $116.50 ($112 plus a $4.50 Safety Codes Council fee) as of September 2026 — and it is not the permit that applies to you, because you cannot pull it for gas work. Contractor trade permits are priced on the value of the work. Ask to see the permit as its own line on the invoice.

Before you sign anything

A tankless unit is rated in flow at a given temperature rise, not in litres of storage — so how big it really is depends on the water temperature arriving at your house in the worst month of the year.

We could not verify a Calgary winter mains water temperature from any primary source. The City does not appear to publish one, and we will not lift a figure off an American sizing calculator and dress it up as local. Ask 311 — and make the installer commit in writing to the inlet temperature they sized on. If it is a summer number, the unit is smaller than you think between November and March.

Then get these five itemised separately — they are where the quotes actually differ.

  • Gas. Model, maximum input, total meter load, pipe size and length — so you know whether the upsize is priced or discovered later.
  • Venting. Which wall it exits, run length and elbows, and who deals with the old chimney — so the hole in your house is a decision you made.
  • Condensate. Where it drains and whether a neutralizer is included — so the acidic discharge is handled, not quietly pointed at your floor drain.
  • Electrical. Whether the 120 V outlet exists or is being added — so it is priced now, not as a change order on install day.
  • Isolation valves and water treatment. Both, quoted separately — so you can flush it yourself, and so the hardness warranty question has an answer on paper.

And before any of it, make sure the old tank is actually the problem — a failed thermocouple is a cheap repair, not a reason to spend thousands. If there is no hot water this week rather than a plan for next month, start at the no hot water page; if water is spreading across the floor, shut the supply off and call.
